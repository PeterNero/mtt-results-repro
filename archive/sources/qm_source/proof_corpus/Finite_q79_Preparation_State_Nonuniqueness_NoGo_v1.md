# Finite q79 Preparation-State Nonuniqueness No-Go

## Question

Do the exact q79 finite symmetry, complex structure and normalized Hessian
select a unique quantum preparation state?

## Theorem

Let `rho(S3)` be the diagonal two-copy permutation representation on

\[
V=\mathbb C^3_D\oplus\mathbb C^3_E.
\]

Its commutant has complex dimension eight. Adding the exact complex structure

\[
J_{DE}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-I_3\\I_3&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

reduces the commutant to complex dimension four. Adding the normalized Hessian
`Q=I-P_Haar` does not reduce it further.

In particular, on the two-dimensional zero-Hessian sector, the operators

\[
K_\pm=\frac12\left(P_{\rm Haar}\mp iJ_{DE}P_{\rm Haar}\right)
\]

are distinct rank-one Hermitian projectors. Both commute with the `S3` action,
`J_DE`, and `Q`, and both have zero normalized Hessian energy.

Therefore the finite q79 geometry does not select a unique density operator or
pure preparation state.

## Proof

Each permutation copy decomposes as `1 + std`, so the two-copy carrier is

\[
(\mathbf1\otimes\mathbb C^2)\oplus
(\mathrm{std}\otimes\mathbb C^2).
\]

Schur decomposition gives commutant

\[
M_2(\mathbb C)\oplus M_2(\mathbb C),
\]

of complex dimension eight. On each multiplicity space, commuting with `J_DE`
leaves the two-dimensional complex algebra generated by `I` and `J_DE`, giving
total dimension four. The Hessian is scalar on the trivial and standard
isotypic sectors, so it adds no condition. Exact matrix multiplication verifies
the displayed `K_+` and `K_-` identities.

## Interpretation

This is not a defect. Quantum theories require preparation-dependent states.
The result shows that state choice is genuine state data, not a continuous
theory-fitting knob. A Born source theorem must therefore provide a covariant
map

\[
\text{physical preparation }x\longmapsto \rho_x
\]

and matching context-indexed basin measures. It should not seek one universal
state selected solely by the finite geometry.
