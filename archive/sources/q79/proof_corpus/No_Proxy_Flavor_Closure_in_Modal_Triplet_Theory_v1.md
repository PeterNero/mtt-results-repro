# Purpose and scope

This paper addresses the remaining open bottleneck in the corrected
Theta-Closure and Execution program.

The corrected Tier-4 flavor paper establishes an explicit real benchmark:
printed Yukawa and Majorana matrices reproduce quark masses, CKM magnitudes,
neutrino splittings, and PMNS magnitudes up to rounding.  That is an existence
result.  It is not yet a closed derivation, because the matrices are still
specified at the level of entries.

The purpose of the present paper is to define the stricter test required by the
MTT parameter-closure contract:

> Flavor observables must descend from shared holonomy/localization bottleneck
> data, not from independent entry-wise matrix choices.

We do not claim in this paper that the bottleneck solve is complete.  Instead,
we formulate the exact closure problem, identify what data may be used, and
state the pass/fail criteria.

# Relation to the tiered MTT program

The tiered program separates four kinds of result:

- Tier 1: topology-only constraints, exact under bundle assumptions.

- Tier 2: geometry-light identities and bounds.

- Tier 3: calibrated latent overlap data from measured couplings.

- Tier 4: explicit geometric or string-lift realization.

The earlier Execution II paper belongs to Tier 4.  It shows that a realistic
flavor benchmark can coexist with the calibrated gauge corner.  This paper is a
closure audit of that Tier-4 result.  It asks whether the benchmark is merely
realizable, or whether it is generated by the same small bottleneck data that
the MTT corpus identifies as the remaining source of flavor freedom.

# The no-proxy principle

The parameter-closure paper in the MTT corpus forbids proxy knobs in a closed
formulation.  We restate the rule in the language of flavor.

#### Definition

A *proxy flavor knob* is any adjustment that changes a flavor observable while
not being determined by the shared MTT bottleneck data.

The following are proxy knobs:

1.  entry-wise Yukawa rescalings;

2.  independently chosen CKM or PMNS angles;

3.  independently fitted CP phases;

4.  sector-local instanton corrections not fixed by common geometry or
    topology;

5.  separate quark and lepton localization choices with no common upstream
    constraint.

#### Definition

A flavor construction is *no-proxy closed* if all quark, lepton, neutrino, and
CP observables are functions of a single bottleneck datum
$$
\mathcal B_{\mathrm{fl}}
=
\bigl(
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{loc}},
\{L_{ij}\},
\{\rho_\gamma\},
\mathcal W,
\mathcal I
\bigr),
$$
where:

- $\mathcal G_{\mathrm{loc}}$ is the allowed localization graph of zero modes;

- $L_{ij}$ are the pairwise overlap line bundles;

- $\rho_\gamma$ are flat holonomy characters subject to the phase sum rule;

- $\mathcal W$ are wavefunction width data fixed by the calibrated geometry;

- $\mathcal I$ is the finite set of allowed instanton or exceptional-cycle
  classes.

No separate entry-wise matrix parameters are allowed.

#### Closure map

The desired flavor closure map is
$$
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{MTT}}:
\mathcal B_{\mathrm{fl}}
\longrightarrow
\{Y_u,Y_d,Y_e,Y_\nu,M_R\}
\longrightarrow
\{m_f,V_{\mathrm{CKM}},U_{\mathrm{PMNS}},J_{\mathrm{CP}},\Delta m_\nu^2\}.
$$

The first arrow is the nontrivial part.  The second arrow is standard linear
algebra.

# Allowed flavor data

The allowed data are those already present in the MTT topology and geometry
layers.

## Pairwise line bundles

Matter fields live in pairwise overlap sectors built from the three internal
bundles.  The topology-only layer supplies:

$$
L_{12}\otimes L_{23}\otimes L_{31}\cong\mathbb C.
$$

This implies the holonomy phase sum rule
$$
\arg\det U_{12}+\arg\det U_{23}+\arg\det U_{31}
\in 2\pi\mathbb Z.
$$

Therefore quark and lepton phases may not be assigned independently if they
come from the same pairwise bundle structure.

## Localization graph

Let $p_i^{(a)}$ denote the localization center of family $i$ in sector $a$.
In a Gaussian-overlap approximation, a trilinear Yukawa entry has schematic
form
$$
Y^{(a)}_{ij}
=
C_a
\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal I_{ij}^{(a)}}
A_\kappa
\exp\!\left[
-\frac{d_{ij,\kappa}^{\,2}}{\sigma_a^2}
\right]
e^{i\phi_{ij,\kappa}},
$$
where:

- $C_a$ is a sector normalization inherited from the gauge/geometry corner;

- $\mathcal I_{ij}^{(a)}$ is the finite set of allowed overlap channels;

- $d_{ij,\kappa}$ is determined by the localization graph;

- $\sigma_a$ is fixed by local flux/width data;

- $\phi_{ij,\kappa}$ is a holonomy phase subject to the line-bundle sum rule.

The no-proxy requirement is that $d_{ij,\kappa}$ and $\phi_{ij,\kappa}$ are not
chosen independently for each matrix entry.

## Majorana sector

The topology-only layer gives the criterion:

$$
L^{\otimes 2}\cong\mathbb C
$$

for a Majorana mass term.  Thus the right-handed neutrino mass matrix must be
generated only from sectors satisfying this real-structure condition.  If the
condition fails, the benchmark must be Dirac or higher-operator in origin.

# What the current benchmark proves

The corrected Execution II benchmark proves:

- a real matrix benchmark can reproduce CKM and PMNS magnitudes;

- the benchmark can be made reproducible by direct diagonalization;

- neutrino masses can be produced by a seesaw-scale matrix;

- the benchmark does not retune the gauge-sector calibrated data.

It does not prove:

- the matrix entries are forced;

- CP violation is generated;

- quark and lepton phases share a common holonomy origin;

- the localization graph is unique;

- the Majorana scale is fixed by topology rather than selected.

Thus the benchmark passes the Tier-4 existence test, but not yet the no-proxy
closure test.

# No-proxy failure theorem

#### Theorem

If the entries of $Y_u,Y_d,Y_e,Y_\nu$, or $M_R$ can be varied independently
while leaving the calibrated gauge and gravitational bottleneck data unchanged,
then the construction is not no-proxy closed.

#### Proof

No-proxy closure requires all flavor matrices to factor through
$\mathcal B_{\mathrm{fl}}$.  If a matrix entry can be varied independently
without changing $\mathcal B_{\mathrm{fl}}$, then that entry is not a function
of the bottleneck datum.  It is therefore an additional degree of freedom.  By
definition it is a proxy knob, so no-proxy closure fails.

#### Corollary

A printed numerical Yukawa benchmark, by itself, is not a derivation of flavor
closure.  It becomes a derivation only after the benchmark matrices are shown to
arise from $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{MTT}}(\mathcal B_{\mathrm{fl}})$.

# Degree-of-freedom accounting

The Standard Model flavor sector contains many apparent parameters.  The
no-proxy program does not need to remove all physical observables; it must show
that they are outputs of fewer upstream data.

For the real benchmark:

- quark masses supply six singular values;

- CKM magnitudes require three real angles;

- charged leptons supply three singular values;

- neutrino masses supply three singular values;

- PMNS magnitudes require three real angles.

CP adds at least one quark phase and one or more lepton phases.

A fully closed flavor construction must replace these independent choices by:

- a small localization graph;

- a finite list of allowed overlap channels;

- a constrained set of holonomy characters;

- width/flux data inherited from the same geometric corner;

- Majorana admissibility data.

The central quantitative task is therefore:

> Count the dimension or finite cardinality of $\mathcal B_{\mathrm{fl}}$ and
> compare it to the apparent flavor data it generates.

If the bottleneck data have the same effective dimension as arbitrary matrix
entries, no closure has been gained.

# Reconstruction program

The proposed no-proxy flavor closure calculation proceeds in six steps.

## Step 1: Fix the allowed localization graph

Determine the family localization centers from the same pairwise line-bundle
and intersection data used in Tier 4.  The output should be a graph
$\mathcal G_{\mathrm{loc}}$ whose vertices are family wavefunctions and whose
edges are allowed overlap channels.

## Step 2: Fix width and flux data from the gauge corner

Widths $\sigma_a$ must be inherited from the calibrated geometry.  They may not
be independently tuned per entry.

## Step 3: Enumerate allowed instanton or exceptional classes

The set $\mathcal I$ must be finite and topologically specified.  Contributions
may be suppressed or absent, but their existence cannot be chosen entry by
entry.

## Step 4: Impose holonomy phase constraints

All phases must satisfy the determinant sum rule
$$
\phi_{12}+\phi_{23}+\phi_{31}\in 2\pi\mathbb Z.
$$

The quark CKM phase and the lepton CP phases must therefore be related if they
come from the same holonomy datum.

## Step 5: Generate matrices

Compute
$$
(Y_u,Y_d,Y_e,Y_\nu,M_R)
=
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{MTT}}(\mathcal B_{\mathrm{fl}}).
$$

No entry-wise edits are allowed after this step.

## Step 6: Diagonalize and compare

Use standard definitions:
$$
V_{\mathrm{CKM}}=U_u^\dagger U_d,
\qquad
U_{\mathrm{PMNS}}=U_e^\dagger U_\nu,
$$
with
$$
m_\nu=-Y_\nu^T M_R^{-1}Y_\nu v_u^2
$$
when the Majorana criterion is satisfied.

# Pass/fail criteria

## Pass

The no-proxy flavor test passes if:

1.  a single bottleneck datum $\mathcal B_{\mathrm{fl}}$ generates all printed
    matrices;

2.  the generated matrices reproduce observed quark and lepton masses within
    the stated high-scale/RGE uncertainty;

3.  CKM and PMNS magnitudes are reproduced without independent angle fitting;

4.  CP violation is generated by holonomy phases satisfying the MTT phase sum
    rule;

5.  the Majorana or Dirac nature of neutrinos follows from the line-bundle
    criterion rather than from choice.

## Fail

The test fails if any of the following are required:

1.  entry-wise Yukawa edits;

2.  independent quark and lepton phase fitting;

3.  sector-local instanton corrections with no common topological origin;

4.  a Majorana mass matrix incompatible with the topology-only criterion;

5.  a localization graph chosen only to reproduce CKM/PMNS after the fact.

# Conclusion

The corrected Tier-4 flavor benchmark establishes existence, not closure.  The
MTT corpus imposes a stronger standard: flavor must descend from the same
finite bottleneck data that constrain gauge and gravitational sectors.

This paper formulates that standard as a no-proxy closure test.  It identifies
the allowed upstream data, proves that independent matrix-entry choices fail the
test, and gives the reconstruction program required to turn flavor benchmarks
into derived consequences.

The next calculation is therefore sharp:

> Compute $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{MTT}}(\mathcal B_{\mathrm{fl}})$ explicitly and
> determine whether the observed CKM, PMNS, CP, and neutrino data emerge without
> proxy knobs.

If they do, MTT gains a genuinely nontrivial Standard Model closure result.  If
they do not, the strong superset-closure claim fails, even though the structural
and topology-only layers may remain valuable.
